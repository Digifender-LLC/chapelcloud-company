# ADR-0002: Christian operating principles

- **Status:** Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-08-05

## Context

ChapelCloud is explicitly Christian and family-owned. We need durable rules for how faith shapes pricing, marketing tone, support, and vendor choices — so we neither hide the Gospel nor create a “cult vibe” that alienates honest seekers or nonprofit partners. Zero-capital year-1 constraints also require discipline so zeal does not become unfinishable promises (Luke 14:28).

## Decision

We operate by the following principles. They bind internal decisions; public copy stays calm and mission-first per brand guidelines.

1. **Stewardship** — Customer data, uptime, and budgets are entrusted gifts (1 Cor 4:2; 1 Pet 4:10). We design for durability and honesty, not lock-in tricks.
2. **Work as unto the Lord** — Excellence and diligence over hype (Col 3:23–24; Rom 12:11).
3. **Just weights** — Market-rate anchors with per-customer custom quotes; subsidies for smaller ministries are intentional and disclosed in our own records, never bait-and-switch (Prov 11:1).
4. **Quiet witness** — Let good work be visible; avoid performative or overwhelming religious marketing (Matt 5:16). Non-believers should feel respected on chapel.cloud.
5. **Count the cost** — Year 1 is remote-first, integration-first SaaS, no travel-funded reno, no ChMS clone sprint.
6. **Sunday faithfulness with limits** — We cover worship-blocking incidents with severity tiers and family capacity guardrails; we do not pretend infinite heroics are holy.
7. **Primary calling** — God-focused organizations first; other nonprofits welcome when aligned.
8. **Volunteer dignity** — Tech volunteers are partners to train, not obstacles to replace.

## Consequences

- Proposals and SLAs must reference severity tiers and year-1 out-of-scope (travel/full reno).
- Marketing updates (MSP section) follow the calm Christian voice in [../plans/home-site-msp-brief.md](../plans/home-site-msp-brief.md).
- Pricing reviews happen periodically so subsidy does not silently bankrupt the family.
- Violations of these principles are reason to pause a deal, not “growth hacks.”
